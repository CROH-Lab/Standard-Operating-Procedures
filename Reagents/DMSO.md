# 20% Salt-saturated DMSO buffer protocol

## Prepared by: Coral Reef and Ocean Health Research Group: Adapted from Marine Genomics Lab
## Last updated: January 28, 2025
## Pre-requisite SOP: NA
## Safety precautions: **Required PPE - <ins> enclosed shoes, gloves </ins>**

### Purpose: 
Preparing and mixing the chemicals needed to make 2L of DMSO for stable tissue storage for downstream DNA analyses.

[Materials](#Materials)

<a name="Materials"></a> 
### <ins>Materials:</ins>
- 2 L Beaker
- Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO)
- EDTA (MW 292.25)
- Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) pellets
- Sodium chloride (NaCl)
- pH meter
- RODI water 

----

<a name="Procedure"></a> 
### <ins>Procedure:</ins>
1. Dissolve 228.32 g EDTA in 1250 mL of deionized water
  - First, add 1250 mL of DI water to a 2L glass beaker (use plastic 1000 mL graduated cylinder to measure out the water).
  - Place a large stir bar in the beaker.
  - Place beaker on a stir plate.
  - Measure out EDTA powder (100g at a time) in a large weigh boat. SLOWLY add EDTA powder to the swirling water, allowing it to dissolve. Dumping it all in at once causes caking. The solution will be milk white.

2. Place pH meter in the beaker. The pH will be very acidic, around 3.0. Use a spatula to add NaOH pellets. Slowly add the pellets, allowing them to dissolve before adding more, until the pH is exactly 8.0. This is an exothermic reaction, so it will give off some heat and the solution will turn clear. 
3. Add NaCL to saturation. 
4. Double-check that the pH is 8.0. If its below 8.0, add NaOH pellets until it reaches 8.0 again. 
5. Measure out 400 mL DMSO in the plastic graduated cylinder and add it to the solution. 
6. Bring final volume to 2.0 L with RODI water. 
7. Let the solution continue to stir as it cools to room temperature. Once cool, store the DMSO buffer in labeled 1L glass jars.
