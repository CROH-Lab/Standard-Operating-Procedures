## Purpose: 
We have three YSI Sonde’s that can be used to monitor water quality parameters (Temperature, Dissolved Oxygen, Conductivity, pH, and Turbidity). The Professional Pro YSI is a handheld that does not measure Turbidity. The Pro DSS YSI is another handheld YSI that does measure all five parameters. The EXO1 Sonde is a deployable YSI for long-term monitoring.
[Website for turbidity standards for calibration](https://www.ysi.com/product/id-607200/turbidity-standard)

### Materials:
1. Calibration cups, calibration rinsing cup and calibration probe guard
2. 5-gallon bucket filled with tap water and aerator (start 24 hours before calibration)
3. 50 mS/cm  solution
4. pH 4.00 buffer
5. pH 7.00 buffer
6. pH 10.00 buffer
7. DI water
8. 124 NTU turbidity solution
9. CROH Lab YSI Calibration Log
10. Selected sondes for calibration
11. Alcohol
12. Vinegar
13. Krytox
14. Q-tips
15. Kim Wipes
16. Tap Water  

### Equipment:
1. Thermometer
2. YSI

### Cleaning Probes and Ports:
- This should be done quarterly or once a month, depending on how the YSI is acting
- Cleaning will be most important for the EXO1 that is deployed and will experience fouling
- Clean top connector area with vinegar and then alcohol (Qtips and KimWipes)  

Steps:  

1. Take all probes off and wipe all gunk off
2. Vinegar in the port (not in the holes where the pins go)
3. Alcohol to dry it out (end with alcohol)
4. Check O-rings in the probes and replace if need be
5. Krytox then screw back in

### Calibration: 

- For the EXO1 we will need the Core EXO software for calibration, this can be accessed via Bluetooth or an adapter cord.
- The day before calibration: Fill 5-gallon bucket mostly full with tap water. Place aerator in bucket and let run for 24 hours before you begin calibration (or always keep aerator running in 5 gallon bucket) – this is for the ODO calibration.
- Start to fill out the calibration log from the top down, the log is made in the order you should be doing the calibration.
- Only fill calibration cups up to the drawn-on fill line to prevent overflow when sonde is placed in cup.
- Start with ODO: Place selected sondes for calibration in the 5-gallon bucket with tap water and aerator running.
- Record the barometric pressure – you can google this.
- To find the ODO gain: File -> View calibration record -> ODO gain (should be between (0.8-1.7).
- Calibrate Conductivity next, but first time to rinse probes! Put sonde in calibration rinsing cup filled with DI water, shake and swirl, remove sonde, pour conductivity rinse into rinse cal cup and repeat rinsing, remove sonde and place into conductivity cal cup (can rinse with conductivity standard twice if needed).
- Make sure the Standard Value is set to 50.00 mS/cm.
- Calibrate pH next, Move sonde from conductivity cup to rinsing cup filled with DI water, shake and swirl, remove sonde, pour pH 4 rinse into rinsing cup, shake and swirl, remove sonde and place in pH 4 standard cup.
- Next click ADD ANOTHER CAL POINT - Move sonde from pH 4 cup to rinsing cup filled with DI water, shake and swirl, remove sonde, pour pH 7 rinse into rinsing cup, shake and swirl, remove sonde and place in pH 7 standard cup, repeat for pH 10.
- When calibrating pH the temperature should always be at 25C – check this and record on the log, this can impact your pH reading - Record pH 4, pH 7 and pH 10 Post Calibration Values, pH mV values, and temperature.
- Lastly calibrate turbidity (for EXO1 and Pro DSS only), Leave sonde in DI calibration cup, Click small arrow next to Turbidity rectangle, Click CALIBRATE button for Turbidity (FNU), Once data stabilizes click APPLY, Click ADD ANOTHER CAL POINT.
- Move sonde from DI cal cup to rinsing cup filled with Turbidity standard 124/126 NTU, shake and swirl, remove sonde and place sonde in Cal cup filled with Turbidity 124 NTU, Once data stabilizes click APPLY (record probe serial number), Click COMPLETE CALIBRATION, Record Turbidity 0 and 124 NTU.
- Congratulations your sonde is now calibrated! All calibration logs should be stored in the YSI calibration binder in the lab above the computer.
- Important Notes: If Calibration out of range, do not continue. If you think you did a bad calibration, or weird readings, reset it to factory calibration settings and try again.
- Calibrate either every other week if using often or once a month at minimum. 

### Storage: 

- Store in Tap Water (not seawater and not DI), only a small amount at the bottom, doesn’t need to be touching the probes just needs the moisture from the liquid.
- Rinse with tap water after using every time! 

### Quality Assurance and Control Methods
Proper protocols and training must be implemented to ensure the quality of data generated in the laboratory. Quality control measures are outlined in each protocol to ensure data generated are of accurate quality. Researchers must ensure that all equipment is accurately calibrated, inspected, and maintained according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Data must be entered in Excel spreadsheets by one individual and QA-QC checked by a different individual.   

_Data Review_

All laboratory data will be reviewed for completeness and transfer errors. Data will be reviewed by a second individual after entry into Excel spreadsheets by comparing the entered, electronic data to the original records (e.g., hand-written datasheets or laboratory notebooks).  Data will be summarized as descriptive statistics and in tabular and graphical form to allow visual inspection and verification, and comparison to expected or target values.      

_Data Verification_  

Data will be checked for compliance with the procedures outlined in the SOPs and OPs.  Any deviations from those procedures and the impact on the quality of the data will be assessed and discussed with Task Members. Any laboratory data outliers will be flagged.  

_Data Validation_ 

Once the data has been reviewed and verified, it will be assessed to determine the overall acceptability of the objectives of the project.  Blank samples, such as with water quality testing, will be used to determine any biases or instrument calibration issues during the sample collection and analysis processes.  Control samples will be used to determine the condition of the experimental test specimens in the absence of experimental treatments or exposures.  Any errors in datasets detected will be discussed with lab members and project leads to determine the impact on the data and its use for the project.  If there are any limitations to the data, they will be disclosed as part of the published literature.  

_**Procedure Specific QA/QC Methods**_ 

_YSI Equipment_

The YSI will be properly cleaned before and after each use to prevent degradation of the probes and misreads. Any complications with the equipment will be handled by trained and qualified technicians. All corrective actions will be reported in the equipment log.  

_Reagents_

All reagents, buffers, and chemicals will be used within date. Appropriate documentation for each chemical will be recorded and maintained (CAS No., expiry dates, and hazard warnings, etc.) Any contaminated reagents will be discarded to prevent interference with the experiments.
