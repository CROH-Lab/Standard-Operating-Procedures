### Setting up PAM using a computer:

1. Turn on the laptop computer and plug in the USB to computer.
- Open WinControl software – this will turn on PAM 
The software will search for an instrument connection. If it is not available, you will have to close it by choosing CANCEL in the dialog box and try again with the cable plugged in. This software, or the computer, may need to be updated for the program to work. When downloading WinControl, make sure you download the driver with the program. There may be an error message if the USB port doesn’t read the PAM device because there is no driver. Try re-downloading WinControl from the internet.

Launch again. It should recognize the connection with the Diving-PAM. If yes, it will turn the PAM on and the green display on the PAM will illuminate. 

2. Take measurements at the same time every day, the amount of light the coral has been exposed to throughout the day will change the values. Place corals in a dark room to acclimate for a minimum of 20-30 minutes before taking measurements.
- Use a bubbler to aerate the corals and turn on red light. You can see with red light, but it is not strong enough to drive photosynthesis.
- Dark acclimation for 20 minutes allows all of the chlorophyll a reaction centers to relax for optimal fluorescence measurements. Do not allow light from the computer to get too close to the corals.
- Set up a seawater tank into which corals will be placed for PAM measurements, you do not need to aerate in this tank.

3. Go to the Settings tab, the only settings you should change are the Measuring Light Intensity (Int), Gain under System Parameter, and the F-offset adjustment.
- __F-offset__: Place the probe in the seawater with no coral in it, this water should be taken from the tank the coral came from and adjust the F-offset. Adjust between each coral fragment, but record the F-offset before each RLC to monitor values over time and between samples.
- __Intensity & Gain__: Next check your Gain and Intensity, they should be low to start. A higher gain and intensity would over-saturate the coral and will leave you with a “grainy picture.” For example, with a bleached coral you will need these values to be higher. The higher the gain and intensity, the more “noise” in the measurement and it will decrease the reliability of the data. Record this value for each sample.
- __Ft__: To confirm your gain and intensity values are where they need to be for that coral fragment, check the Ft value in the Online section highlighted below, place the probe on the coral, the Ft should be 200-500, with 300-400 being the optimal range. The Ft value should not fluctuate more than +/- 10 when the probe is on the coral. This value should be similar for each coral measured as a baseline background fluorescence. Ft is a value representing the background fluorescence when measuring, it is the background noise that will impact the accuracy of the reading. The Ft is measured by the flickering red light on the probe, it’s measuring the background signal from the functioning chloroplasts, so this may change based on light/dark acclimation and the time of day you conduct measurements. Record this value before the start of each RLC.
- __Measuring distance__: Another way to adjust the Ft is by changing the measuring distance by moving the plastic tubing covering the probe. The farther the probe is from the coral, the lower the Ft will be. You should try to be consistent with measuring distance between samples, change Gain & Intensity first before moving plastic cover. The plastic cover should always leave at least 1cm distance between coral and probe. Be careful not to scratch or puncture the probe. Record if you change the distance.

4. Choose a coral to measure and record the ID number on the datasheet
- Place coral in the measuring tank and find a place on the coral surface where the probe can be flush at 90 degrees to the coral. Do not measure tips of coral.
- When placing the probe in the water, go in at an angle to remove any air bubbles that may get caught in the rubber tubing on the probe, these air bubbles will affect the measurement
- The probe has a rubber tube around the tip that extends ~1cm. This distance from the probe and coral should be kept consistent unless measuring a stressed coral and trying to adjust the Ft (see above).
- This position needs to be held for 90 seconds while the RLC runs. Do NOT allow the probe to move. It is best to position the probe in a way that it will stay positioned on its own without having to hold it while it’s measuring, this avoids human error from shaking hands or moving the probe.
- Depending on the size of the coral being measured you will have to find 2-3 areas to measure that do not overlap in measuring space. Once the red light from the probe is shown on the coral, that area has been light activated and the coral would need to rest in the dark for 15 minutes to close the reaction centers before being measured again. Flip the coral over, to measure the other side that was not exposed to light, for the second and third measurements. If that is not possible, put that fragment back in the dark and go back 15 minutes later to re-measure.

5. Go to the Light Curve tab in the software and click on Start LC.
- Change the Y-axis units to ETR (this is optional but helpful for viewing)
- Autoscale the Y-axis after the first RLC or during the curve to show all values (ok to do any time)
- Make sure the PAR, Y(II), ETR, and NPQ boxes are checked in the sidebar on the right. Several others will already be checked (T, F, Fm’), leave those. You can always go back and look at all values later.
- Look at the Chart (highlighted below) when conducting measurements and make sure there is a spike and flat plateau and then drop, you don’t want a graph that fluctuates up and down, that means something is wrong.

6. Go to __Report__ tab once the light curve has finished measuring.
- Record the time at PAR = 0 and the Fv/Fm value (this is same as first Y(II) value)
- Record the line numbers (ex = 1-10) to easily read the CSV with exported data.
- Return to Light Curve tab and record the rest of the variables (NPQ, ETR, etc.) from the results section on the right.
- Autoscale the RLC to find the ETRm (max saturation for the electron transport rate) and the corresponding PAR value. This value shows you that at light levels (PAR) any higher, there is too much light and photo-inhibition is occurring. Light energy is then dissipated as heat and non-photochemical quenching (NPQ) occurs.
- You see on the graph below that when the ETRm is reached, the NPQ slope continues to increase as non-photochemical quenching is occurring, there is too much light inhibiting photosynthesis (this level is reached around 600-800 PAR for most Hawaii corals) 

7. Repeat steps 3-6 for all coral samples.
- Gently remove any coral mucus from the probe with your finger between samples and check for air bubbles.
- Note whether F-offset strays and return to the Settings tab to Adjust periodically as needed 

*You may move through the tabs in WinControl during RLCs without causing disruption of the measurements

8. When all of the measurements have been taken, return to the Light Curve tab. Then go to the File in the main menu at top left and scroll down to Save Data. Name the file with the date and save in the appropriate folder.
- The data can be saved as a WinControl file or a CSV file. WinControl files will save all the data but can only be opened will the Win Control software. To save the data in .txt format (or .csv) go to the Report tab and make sure all the desired data type boxes are checked, all the variables you want recorded, then click on Options at top right of the window and scroll down to Export All.
- It is recommended to save all data as a WinControl and CSV file.
- Name the file with the date and save it in the same folder.
- For lab projects, please upload copies of these files to the appropriate folder on the OneDrive. Always have your data backed up in 3 locations.  

9. Shutting down procedures are as follows:
- Double check that all files are saved
- Close the WinControl software
- Unplug the USB connection to the Diving-PAM
- On the Diving-PAM, touch the OFF “button” and verify that the green light is off
- Close computer, turn ON room light, turn OFF red light, leave PAM and computer in charge
- Rinse in fresh water any of the components that were in contact with seawater. Generally, this is just the probe. Probe can be submerged in a beaker of water for several minutes
- Leave probe and PAR sensor fiber optics in such a way as to avoid kinking the cables. Cover the PAM with a towel

### __Some tips:__
- Y is the yield, or the capacity of the algae to fluoresce, this capacity is high at the start, all reaction centers are open and ready to fluorescence, as light is added the capacity decreases because the algae are fluorescing therefore the “capacity” is lower since it’s already happening. Same with Y(NPQ) that is the capacity or yield to do non-photochemical quenching.
- The battery will get weaker with repeated measurements. At its fullest, the value hovers around 13. Note that the battery does not get too low while taking measurements. Anything below 10 or 11 could affect measurements.
- The SAT-Pulse chart shows a curve that should plateau at the top dotted line (this line represents Fm’ -- the maximal fluorescence in light). In most cases, if there is no plateau and instead a skew, it is possible the probe is not at 90 degrees to the sample. Check this.
- If you make a mistake with the probe placement, it slips, or for any reason you wish to take another measurement on a sample, that sample must be re-acclimated in the dark for a minimum of 20 minutes.

### Troubleshooting 

- If you get an error message about signal overflow, try decreasing the Gain* value or Meas. Light intensity. Adjust the f-offset anytime you change the Gain* or Meas. Light Intensity.

###Quality Assurance and Control Measures 

__*Proper Training*__

Proper protocols and training must be implemented to ensure the quality of data generated in the laboratory. Researchers must ensure that all equipment is accurately calibrated, inspected, and maintained according to the manufacturer’s instructions.  

 

__*Data Review*__

All laboratory data will be reviewed for completeness and transfer errors. Data will be reviewed by a second individual after entry into Excel spreadsheets by comparing the entered, electronic data to the original records (e.g., hand-written datasheets or laboratory notebooks).  Data will be summarized as descriptive statistics and in tabular and graphical form to allow visual inspection and verification, and comparison to expected or target values.   



__*Data Verification*__

Data will be checked for compliance with the procedures outlined in the SOPs and OPs.  Any deviations from those procedures and the impact on the quality of the data will be assessed and discussed with Task Members. Any laboratory data outliers will be flagged. 

 

__*Data Validation*__

Once the data has been reviewed and verified, it will be assessed to determine the overall acceptability of the objectives of the project.  Blank samples, such as with water quality testing, will be used to determine any biases or instrument calibration issues during the sample collection and analysis processes.  Control samples will be used to determine the condition of the experimental test specimens in the absence of experimental treatments or exposures.  Any errors in datasets detected will be discussed with lab members and project leads to determine the impact on the data and its use for the project.  If there are any limitations to the data, they will be disclosed as part of the published literature. 

 

__*Procedure Specific QA/QC Methods*__

The photosynthetic efficiency of each coral will be assessed using a PAM Fluorometer (Diving-PAM 2.0, Walz). Photosynthesis to irradiance (P-I) curves, chlorophyll fluorescence, and photosystem II energy conversion are created for each coral. PAM measurements are extremely effective in detecting stress before visual responses are seen. Also, PAM measurements can be done on live corals and, therefore, non-destructive. Seawater within the probe tank should be changed to match the treatment seawater of the coral being analyzed.
